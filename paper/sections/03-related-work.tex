\paragraph{Energy efficiency of LLM-generated code.}
Solovyeva et al.~\cite{solovyeva2025powerhungry} analyse the energy efficiency and
performance of code generated by GitHub Copilot, GPT-4o, and o1-mini for ``hard''
LeetCode problems in Python, Java, and C++ on two platforms (a Mac and a PC). They
report that the models are considerably more successful at generating correct Python
and Java code than C++ code.
Islam et al.~\cite{islam2025evaluating} evaluate code generated by 20 LLMs for 878
LeetCode problems against canonical human-written solutions and find that LLM-generated
solutions are frequently less energy-efficient, with large differences between models
and algorithmic categories.
Apsan et al.~\cite{apsan2025whereare} compare Python code generated by six LLMs with four
prompting techniques against human-written solutions and those of a green-software
expert on nine EvoEval problems, measuring energy on a server, a PC, and a Raspberry~Pi.
They observe that the relative efficiency of LLM-generated versus human code, as well as
the most effective prompting technique, varies with the hardware platform.

\paragraph{Energy efficiency of programming languages.}
Pereira et al.~\cite{pereira2017energy} established that languages differ substantially
in energy, time, and memory, and that these three measures are related but not
interchangeable.
