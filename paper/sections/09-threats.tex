\paragraph{Access via coding CLIs.}
Models are accessed through their vendors' coding CLIs, whose built-in system prompts we
cannot inspect or align, and whose defaults (e.g., sampling, reasoning effort) differ. The
factor \emph{LLM} thus measures model and tool jointly. We mitigate agentic effects by
disabling tools, ignoring user and project configuration, and logging every tool
invocation; Codex CLI offers no documented switch to disable tools, so it runs in a
read-only sandbox instead. CLIs and models evolve quickly, so we pin CLI versions and
record model identifiers and dates. With three levels of the LLM factor, rank-based
analyses (\RQ{4}) have limited resolution; the interaction terms of the mixed-effects
model are therefore the primary evidence.

\paragraph{Confounding of model and implementation.}
The effect of an LLM cannot be fully separated from the concrete algorithm it chooses;
these strategy differences are, however, part of the phenomenon under study.

\paragraph{Data contamination.}
The EffiBench-X tasks were released between November 2023 and October 2024 and the CLBG
tasks and their solutions have been public for many years; all studied LLMs were likely
trained on them. Memorised solutions may therefore inflate correctness and affect
efficiency. Since all LLMs are exposed to the same tasks, this threat primarily concerns
absolute results rather than the comparison between models; the contrast between
